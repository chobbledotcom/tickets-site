      A single-operator booking site takes bookings for stock one operator
      hires out. A marketplace lets multiple owners list stock. Depending on
      its model, it may collect a commission and arrange payments to owners.
      These are different businesses with different costs and risks.

      This guide sets out the distinction, the questions to ask, and where
      Chobble Tickets stops.
  - type: markdown

      ## Two models

      | Question | Single-operator booking site | Multi-vendor marketplace |
      |---|---|---|
      | Whose stock is listed? | The operator's own | Many owners or vendors |
      | Who takes the payment? | The operator's payment account | Depends on the marketplace and its payment provider |
      | How can it earn? | The operator earns hire income | Commission, listing fees or subscriptions |
      | Who handles damage, late returns and disputes? | The operator | Depends on the marketplace's terms |
      | Who controls the customer relationship? | The operator | Depends on the marketplace's terms |
      | What functions may be needed? | A catalogue, checkout and booking record | Vendor onboarding, payouts, commission and dispute handling |

      A marketplace may need records of payouts, identity checks, terms
      between the parties and a process for disputes. The software, payment
      provider and operator may each handle different parts.

      ## When a marketplace is the right choice

      - You do not own the stock and want other people to list it.
      - You plan to earn a commission on each rental.
      - You can handle vendor onboarding, identity checks and payouts.
      - You can write terms that cover the owner, the renter and the
        platform.
      - You can support disputes over damage, late returns and refunds.
      - You have the marketing budget to attract both owners and renters.
      - You need a catalogue that includes stock from other owners.

      ## When a single-operator site is the right choice

      - You own the stock and want to hire it out.
      - You want payments to go straight to your own Stripe, Square or
        SumUp account.
      - You want to keep the customer relationship and the data.
      - You do not want to run vendor payouts or commission accounting.
      - You need dated bookings, part-payments and delivery run sheets.
      - You want a hosting price that does not rise with sales.

  - type: markdown

      ## Questions to ask before buying marketplace software

      1. Does the price include vendor payouts, or is that a separate
         integration?
      2. Who is the merchant of record for each rental?
      3. How is commission calculated, and on what total?
      4. Who handles refunds, chargebacks and damage disputes?
      5. Can an owner export their stock and customer data?
      6. What identity checks does the platform require, and who performs
         them?
      7. What happens to the marketplace if the software company stops
         trading?
      8. Does the licence cover updates, or are they charged separately?

      A vendor-authored comparison is not a substitute for a written answer
      to those questions.
