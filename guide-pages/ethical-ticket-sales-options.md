      There is no single test for whether a ticketing provider is ethical.
      This guide lists documented company structures, certifications, funding
      models and source licences. It does not give providers an overall ethical
      score.

      A charitable structure does not remove per-ticket fees. B Corp
      certification does not determine how attendee data is used. Open source
      code does not, on its own, say who owns a company or where its income
      goes.

      Some providers publish a pledge to donate a share of their booking fee
      or profit instead of, or alongside, a company structure. The guide to
      [where ticketing money goes](/guide/choosing-a-ticket-platform/where-ticketing-money-goes/)
      sets out how to read those pledges against the fee they are taken from.

      ## Purpose-locked and charitable organisations

      ### Chobble Tickets

      [Chobble Tickets](/about/) is provided by Chobble CIC, a UK community
      interest company limited by guarantee without share capital, so it has
      no shares to sell. Its asset lock restricts how company assets can be
      transferred, and a CIC can stop being a CIC only by dissolving or by
      becoming a charity. Chobble's social
      impact page says it donates 10% of income to the Against Malaria
      Foundation; the linked foundation fundraiser describes the policy as 10%
      of profits.

      Chobble's income comes from flat hosting fees and technical work, not
      advertising or attendee data sales. The complete ticketing product is
      open source and can be [self-hosted](/hosting/).

      Managed hosting costs £50/year or £5/month, with a £25 annual price for
      charities, community groups, artists and musicians. Chobble adds no
      [per-ticket platform fee](/features/no-per-ticket-fees/), while payment
      providers charge separately.

      ### Humanitix

      [Humanitix](/compared-to/humanitix/) is a registered charity. It states
      that 100% of its profits go to education, healthcare and community
      projects.

      The service is funded by a percentage and fixed fee on each paid ticket.
      It has a lower rate for registered charities and schools, and free events
      have no fee.

      Humanitix also runs an event-discovery marketplace and makes personalised
      recommendations. It stores attendee data on its servers. Its privacy
      notice says direct marketing is sent only when an attendee explicitly
      opts in.

      ### Dandelion

      [Dandelion](/compared-to/dandelion/) is provided by Symbiota Ltd.
      Symbiota describes itself as a not-for-profit worker co-operative, and
      Companies House records it as a company limited by guarantee without
      share capital.

      Dandelion has no annual subscription or mandatory fee on tickets sold
      through the platform. It normally asks organisers for a voluntary 1%
      contribution, asks buyers for donations, charges for some events that
      send buyers elsewhere, and sells boosts in its public listings.

      Its marketplace creates platform profiles and uses past bookings and
      shared gatherings to recommend events and people. Its source can be
      inspected and run for internal use, but new versions cannot be used to
      offer a competing commercial service for two years.

      ## Certified B Corps

      A certified B Corporation is a company assessed against B Lab's social
      and environmental standards. Certification is different from being a
      charity, co-operative or community interest company.

      ### Ticket Tailor

      [Ticket Tailor](/compared-to/ticket-tailor/) is independently owned and
      B Corp certified. Its current B Lab profile lists no investors.

      It charges a fixed fee for each paid ticket, with lower prices for
      prepaid credits and discounts for charities, PTAs and B Corps. Ticket
      Tailor runs a public Discover events area but states that it does not
      email attendees about other organisers' events.

      ### Resident Advisor

      [Resident Advisor](/compared-to/resident-advisor/) describes itself as
      independent and is B Corp certified. Companies House lists two founders
      as its only active people with significant control, but this does not
      establish complete founder ownership. Its ticketing service is part of a
      wider electronic-music publication and event marketplace.

      Resident Advisor says promoters pay no ticketing fee because buyers cover
      a service fee on each ticket. It does not publish a general rate schedule.
      RA markets platform content and other events through listings,
      recommendations, email and push notifications.

      ## Donation pledges without a purpose-locked structure

      ### Ticketpass

      [Ticketpass](/compared-to/ticketpass/) describes itself as an ethical
      ticketing platform. Its terms record that Fanex Group Ltd acquired the
      Ticketpass business on 9 October 2025 and now operates the platform.
      That company's entry on the Companies House register of people with
      significant control records one person holding 75% or more of the shares
      and voting rights, with the right to appoint and remove directors.

      Paid tickets carry a booking fee and a separate service fee at the same
      published rate, 1.9% + £0.20 each on the Essentials plan and 3.5% +
      £0.35 each on Pro, both excluding VAT. Free events have no fee, and
      organisers can absorb the charges or pass them to buyers.

      Ticketpass donates up to 50% of the booking fee, but not the service
      fee, to a project the organiser or the attendee selects. Its terms
      describe the pledge as a commitment rather than a binding contractual
      obligation. It also runs an event marketplace, requires buyers to create
      an account, and says it may send marketing about Ticketpass and selected
      parties in line with a user's preferences.

      ## Open source and independent projects

      Open source licences let organisers inspect code and may allow them to
      run it themselves. The projects below have relevant ownership or funding
      facts, but the linked comparisons do not record a charitable structure or
      B Corp certification for them.

      - [Pretix](/compared-to/pretix/) describes itself as owner-led and says it
        operates without pressure from investors or creditors. Its Community
        edition is open source, while several enterprise features use paid
        proprietary plugins.
      - [alf.io](/compared-to/swicket/) is an open source community project with
        public finances on Open Collective. Open Collective lists two project
        administrators and Open Source Collective as its fiscal host. Swicket
        is the separate managed enterprise service built on alf.io.
      - [Hi.Events](/compared-to/hi-events/) is operated by Hi.Events Ltd and
        maintained mainly by Dave Earley. Public sources reviewed on 25 July
        2026 did not establish its funding status. Free self-hosting requires
        visible attribution, while removing it requires a commercial licence.

      ## Questions to compare

      - Is the organisation a charity, co-operative, community interest
        company, certified business or standard company?
      - Does income come from subscriptions, per-ticket fees, advertising,
        donations, data sales or a mixture of these?
      - Does the provider market other events to an organiser's attendees?
      - Can organisers export their data, inspect the source code or run the
        software on infrastructure they control?
      - Which useful features, support services and marketplace benefits would
        the organiser give up by choosing another model?
