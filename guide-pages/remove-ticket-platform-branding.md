      "White label" means the booking pages, tickets and emails carry the
      organiser's name instead of the ticketing provider's name. Providers
      treat it in three different ways: included, sold as an extra, or not
      offered at all.

      Branding removal is rarely one setting. It usually needs a logo and
      colour scheme, an organiser-owned web address, and emails sent from the
      organiser's own domain. A provider can supply the first without the other
      two.

      ## The three parts of removing branding

      - **Appearance.** The organiser's logo, colours and text on the booking
        pages. Most reviewed providers offer at least some of this.
      - **The web address.** Whether pages sit on an organiser-owned domain
        such as `tickets.example.org`, a provider subdomain such as
        `example.provider.com`, or a shared provider address.
      - **Emails and tickets.** Whether confirmation emails come from the
        organiser's domain, and whether the provider's name appears on the
        ticket or in the checkout footer.

      A provider can be described as white label while still serving pages from
      its own domain. This guide records the qualifications where the reviewed
      sources state them.

      ## Organiser branding without full removal

      These providers apply the organiser's logo, colours or content but keep
      the service on their own addresses, or do not document complete removal.

      - [TicketSource](/compared-to/ticketsource/) takes the organiser's logo
        and colour scheme and can embed the ticket shop in the organiser's
        site, but serves it from TicketSource domains. No custom-domain option
        was documented.
      - [Weezevent](/compared-to/weezevent/) applies the organiser's logo,
        colours and typography to a mini-site, or embeds the booking module in
        the organiser's website. Organiser-owned domains were not established.
      - [EVENTIM.Light](/compared-to/eventim-light/) supports organiser
        branding on ticket shops, which remain on the EVENTIM.Light service.
      - [Tessera Tickets](/compared-to/tessera-tickets/) allows custom HTML and
        CSS, though complete removal of provider branding was not established.
      - [PTA Events](/compared-to/pta-events/) supplies a branded fundraising
        website, though complete removal of provider branding was not
        established.
      - [Pretix Hosted](/compared-to/pretix/) supports organiser branding and
        offers custom domains by arrangement.
      - [Cheddar Up](/compared-to/cheddar-up/) includes a Brand Kit on its Team
        plan, at $48/month or $420/year. Complete removal of Cheddar Up
        branding was not established, and pages stay on Cheddar Up addresses.
      - [Ticketpass](/compared-to/ticketpass/) lists custom branding and custom
        tickets on its Enterprise plan. Whether Ticketpass branding can be

      ## Questions to ask a provider

      - Does the base price include branding removal, or is it an add-on or a
        higher plan?
      - Can the booking pages use a domain the organiser owns, or only a
        provider subdomain?
      - Do confirmation emails come from the organiser's domain, or from the
        provider's?
      - Does the provider's name appear on the ticket, the checkout footer or
        the refund emails?
      - If the software is self-hosted, does its licence require a visible
        attribution link?

      Prices and plan names change. Each linked comparison page lists the
      sources used, and most record the date the information was checked.
      Where no date is recorded, check the provider's own pricing page before
      relying on the figures.
