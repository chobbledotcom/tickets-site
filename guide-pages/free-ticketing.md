      "Free ticketing" can mean three different things. It can describe
      software that costs nothing to run, a plan that charges the organiser
      nothing, or tickets sold with nothing added to the price. A platform
      can be free for the organiser and still take money from the buyer.

      This guide describes how ticketing platforms with no price pay for
      themselves, and it lists the ways to pay Chobble less, or nothing at
      all.

      ## How ticketing platforms with no price pay for themselves

      A platform that charges some organisers nothing still has an income.
      The reviewed models fall into three groups:

      - **Buyer contributions.** [Zeffy](/compared-to/zeffy/) charges
        nonprofits no subscription and no fee per ticket. Each buyer is
        asked for an optional contribution at checkout. The request appears
        twice, and about 60% of donors leave one.
      - **Fees on paid tickets.** [Eventbrite](/compared-to/eventbrite/)
        publishes events for free, and free events carry no fees. Paid
        tickets in the UK cost 6.95% + £0.59 each.
      - **Limited free plans.** Some providers cap what a free plan can
        sell, or hold features back for paid tiers. The
        [fee disguise guide](/guide/choosing-a-ticket-platform/how-ticketing-platforms-disguise-fees/)
        collects the patterns, from free-event headlines to buyer-paid fees.

      Each figure comes from a comparison page that records the date it
      was checked. "Free" describes the organiser's invoice, not the
      buyer's receipt.

      ## What Chobble Tickets charges

      Chobble Tickets has no free plan. Its income comes from prepaid
      managed hosting, at £50 a year or £5 a month.

      Nothing is added to a sale. There is no per-ticket platform fee, no
      feature tiers, and no charge that rises with volume. Ticket income
      goes straight to the organiser's own Stripe, Square or SumUp account.
      Chobble is a community interest company funded by those annual fees,
      so its income does not change with what an event sells.

      Free listings behave like paid ones minus the payment. A listing
      priced at zero needs no payment provider, takes sign-ups with
      capacity and questions, and records no booking fee. Hosting is still
      prepaid, so a site used only for free events costs the same as any
      other.

      ## Paying Chobble less, or nothing

      - **A trial month.** A free month of managed hosting is available to
        anyone who asks through the
        [contact page](https://www.chobble.com/contact). The trial site has
        every feature switched on.
      - **The community rate.** Charities, community groups, artists and
        musicians pay half the annual price, £25 a year. The discount
        applies to annual billing, not to monthly billing.
      - **Self-hosting.** The complete product is published under the
        AGPL-3.0 licence, so the software costs nothing. A
        self-hosted operator pays instead for a server, an email provider
        and payment processing. The [hosting page](/hosting/) and the
        [deployment guide](/features/deployment/) set out the options.
      - **Read-only after a term.** If a prepaid term ends without
        renewal, the site stays online in read-only mode for 24 months,
        with exports and migration available and nothing owed for the
        read-only months.

      The
      [open source ticketing platforms guide](/guide/choosing-a-ticket-platform/open-source-ticketing-platforms/)
      compares that licence with the terms of other self-installable
      products, and
      [host your own ticket site](/guide/choosing-a-ticket-platform/host-your-own-ticket-site/)
      covers running ticketing on infrastructure you control.
