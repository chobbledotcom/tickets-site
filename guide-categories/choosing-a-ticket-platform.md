    content: |
      Choosing a ticket platform comes down to four separate questions: what it
      costs at your volume of sales, who owns the provider, what happens to
      attendee data and where the software runs. The guides below each take one
      of those questions and sort the reviewed providers by it.

      ## Where to start

      For most small organisers, cost is the deciding question. The [cheapest
      ticket sales system](/guide/choosing-a-ticket-platform/cheapest-ticket-sales-system/)
      guide prices a year of 1,000 tickets at £20 on every reviewed provider,
      and shows the sales volume at which a flat annual fee costs less than a
      per-ticket fee. The [guide on disguised fees](/guide/choosing-a-ticket-platform/how-ticketing-platforms-disguise-fees/)
      explains how to read a headline rate before trusting it.

      If you already know the price you can afford, the structure and hosting
      guides narrow the list further. They cover charitable and co-operative
      providers, UK-registered companies, branding removal, custom domains and
      software you can run yourself.

      ## How the guides are written

      Chobble, the community interest company that runs Chobble Tickets, writes
      these guides and appears in them as one of the providers. Every guide
      names the rule it uses to include a provider, and notes where a service
      has voluntary charges, paid feature tiers, branding rules or separate
      payment-processing costs. Each provider entry links to a full comparison
      with its sources and the date it was checked.
