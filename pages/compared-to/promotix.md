      # Chobble Tickets vs PromoTix

      PromoTix is a US ticketing and event-marketing platform that describes its ticketing as fee-free for organisers. This page compares it to Chobble Tickets.
  - type: markdown
    content: |
      ## Open source & flat fee PromoTix alternative

      PromoTix sells its ticketing as costing the organiser nothing. On the
      free plan the charge moves to the attendee, who pays 3.5% + £1.19 on
      every paid ticket. On the Professional plan that fee can be removed by
      buying an annual subscription, and PromoTix does not publish what that
      subscription costs.

      Chobble Tickets charges the organiser £50/year or £5/month and charges
      nobody a per-ticket fee. The source code is published under the
      AGPL-3.0-only licence, so anyone can read it, fork it, or run their own
      copy. PromoTix does not publish its source code.
  - type: markdown

      ## Pricing comparison

      [PromoTix](https://www.promotix.com/pricing-gbp) lists two plans on its
      GBP pricing page. Ticketing + Marketing carries no organiser
      subscription and charges attendees 3.5% + £1.19 a paid ticket.
      Professional Enterprise removes that fee in exchange for a customised
      annual subscription, and the page asks you to request a price rather
      than showing one.

      On the free plan that fee comes to £1.54 on a £10 ticket, £1.72 on a
      £15 ticket and £1.89 on a £20 ticket. As a share of the ticket price
      that is 15.4%, 11.4% and 9.5%, because the £1.19 stays the same however
      cheap the ticket is. Adding your own service fee on top costs a further
      £0.99 a paid ticket.

      Chobble Tickets charges a flat £50/year or £5/month, or £25/year for
      charities, community groups, artists and musicians on the annual plan.
      There are [no per-ticket platform fees](/features/no-per-ticket-fees/)
      and no feature tiers. Against the PromoTix free plan, the £50 is repaid
      after about 33 tickets at £10, 30 at £15 or 27 at £20.

      Payment processing is separate on both platforms. PromoTix's support
      page says it charges nothing for processing and that Stripe does, and
      Stripe's UK rate is 1.5% + 20p a payment. Chobble Tickets connects to
      the organiser's own [Stripe, Square or SumUp](/features/stripe-and-square/)
      account on the same basis.

      ## The other PromoTix charges

      Several PromoTix features carry their own rate on top of the ticket fee.
      An organiser who uses them should add these to the total.

      - Merchandise and add-ons cost 10% of the sale
      - Ambassador transactions cost 10% on the Professional plan
      - Unsecured streaming costs £0.75 a viewer
      - Secured streaming costs £0.99 a viewer per two hours at 720p, or
        £1.39 at 1080p, plus £5 an event to set up
      - Push notifications cost £50 to set up and £0.01 each
      - PromoTix Reseller, the secondary resale marketplace, charges 10% or
        $1.99, whichever is greater

      The published figures do not always agree with each other. The GBP
      pricing page says attendees pay 3.5% + £1.19, while the support page
      lists event ticketing as "0% to 3% depending upon plan".

      The fee for passing on your own service charge is also written three
      ways: £0.99 a ticket on the GBP pricing table, $0.99 on the support page
      and $1.00 in the pricing page's own FAQ. Check the current figure with
      PromoTix before pricing an event.

      ## Data ownership

      PromoTix is a proprietary platform, and attendee data lives on servers
      it operates. Its privacy policy says personal information may be stored
      and processed in the United States.

      The policy names the safe harbor framework set out by the US Department
      of Commerce as the basis for handling data from the European Union. The
      Court of Justice of the European Union invalidated that framework in
      2015, and two later frameworks have replaced it since.

      The privacy policy reserves broad rights over accounts created on the
      platform. It says PromoTix may market to and solicit anyone as a client
      or member of the application with new opportunities regarding other
      campaigns and shows other users may create. Anyone who buys a ticket to
      your event gets an account, so your attendees fall inside that clause.

      The marketing tools work from the same pool. The PromoTix support page
      says organisers can build custom audiences from contacts in the PromoTix
      database and filter them by age, previous spending history, interests
      and listening habits. That is the other side of the same arrangement:
      other organisers can reach your attendees the same way you reach theirs.

      PromoTix also describes a sponsor arrangement. Where a sponsor pays an
      attendee's ticket service fees, the policy says PromoTix may share the
      attendee's contact information with that sponsor, and that the sponsor
      may contact them later. The policy states that PromoTix does not sell,
      rent or lease its customer lists to third parties.

      With Chobble Tickets, [attendee data is encrypted at rest](/features/encrypted/)
      with the site's public key. An authorised keyed user can decrypt it,
      including an optional recovery owner if enabled. Chobble does not run an
      event marketplace, does not market to attendees, and its income comes
      from the annual fee organisers pay.

      ## Who runs PromoTix?

      PromoTix is operated by PromoTix, Inc., which gives a postal address of

      The company describes itself as founded by event organisers, and says

      17050113, registered in Prestwich, Manchester. All of Chobble's code is
      public under AGPLv3, and the platform can be self-hosted by anyone
      without depending on Chobble as a company.
