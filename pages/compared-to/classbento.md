      # Chobble Tickets vs ClassBento

      ClassBento is a marketplace for creative workshops. Here is how it compares with a flat-fee ticketing platform you can brand as your own.
  - type: markdown
    content: |
      ## Open source & no commission ClassBento alternative

      ClassBento lists your classes on its marketplace and handles the
      booking. It takes no subscription. It takes a commission on each
      sale instead, deducted before your balance is paid to you.

      Chobble Tickets charges one prepaid hosting price, £50/year or
      £5/month, and takes no share of ticket income. Buyers pay your own
      Stripe, Square or SumUp account from the first sale, so the money is
      yours before any platform sees it.

      Chobble Tickets is also [open source](/features/open-source/). The
      complete product is published under the AGPL-3.0-only licence, and
      you can run the same code on your own infrastructure with no Chobble
      software fee. ClassBento publishes no source code and offers no
      self-hosted edition.
  - type: markdown

      ## How ClassBento charges

      ClassBento's UK pricing pages set two rates. A booking that
      originates from your own website, through their booking widget,
      costs 3.9% + £0.25. A booking that originates from the ClassBento
      marketplace costs 20% of the order, inclusive of VAT. Both rates
      cover payment processing, and the teach page says they also cover
      marketing and ClassBento's donations. A Customised tier is priced
      case by case and adds a full website build.

      There are no fixed or monthly fees while your listings stay on the
      marketplace. The pricing page sets one exception: a host who
      excludes all of their listings from the marketplace pays £19 per
      month instead.

      The terms set two rules worth knowing. Commission rates are set
      when a listing is created and may change with notice. And a host
      must not invite customers acquired through the marketplace to book
      through the widget or any route with a different rate, so
      marketplace customers stay at the marketplace rate.

      Listings are reviewed within seven business days before they are
      published, and ClassBento can reject or unpublish a listing.

      ## When you get your money

      ClassBento takes the buyer's payment through its own payment
      provider, deducts its commission, and pays the balance to your bank
      account one business day after the class, or within 14 days for
      orders without a fixed date. Refunds are processed by ClassBento,
      and it also operates a money-back guarantee at its own discretion.

      With Chobble Tickets, no refund of that kind is needed, because the
      money never sits with the platform. Buyers pay your own Stripe,
      Square or SumUp account, and [refunds](/features/refunds/) are
      issued from your admin panel against the same payment.

      ## Where the bookings come from

      ClassBento's income is a share of every sale, so its product is
      demand. The marketplace, the newsletters, the social featuring, the
      copywriting and the handled admin all exist to fill a teacher's
      seats, and a booking that originates there costs 20% of the order,
      inclusive of VAT. For a teacher with no audience of their own, that
      trade can be worth it.

      The contract holds the arrangement in place. A customer acquired
      through the marketplace must keep booking through it at the
      marketplace rate, and the terms stop a host using data collected
      through the platform for direct marketing. Reviews, waitlists,
      loyalty stamps and win-backs gather on the platform's side of the
      relationship, and the booking history a regular customer builds
      lives in ClassBento's system.

      Some of the free parts are tied in as well. Free website hosting
      applies while orders are captured through ClassBento widgets, and
      free expert copywriting is offered if you use the marketplace.

      Chobble Tickets earns its £50/year from the annual fee, so its
      product is the tool, not the demand. The organiser brings the
      audience, from regulars to newsletters to posters, and keeps the
      payment account, the attendee records and the booking history,
      exportable as CSV or as a versioned catalogue another Chobble
      Tickets host can import. The trade is equally plain: nobody markets
      your classes for you.

      ## Data ownership and privacy

      On ClassBento, the platform is the data controller for customer
      data. Its privacy notice says a buyer who has purchased will
      receive marketing unless they opt out, and that ClassBento uses
      past visits to decide which classes and offers to recommend. It
      names Facebook, Instagram, Pinterest, Criteo and Rakuten among the
      advertising and re-targeting providers it works with, and says data
      may be shared with group companies and with an alternative teacher
      if yours cannot fulfil an order.

      Two terms also shape what a host can do with that audience: the
      direct-marketing restriction and the marketplace-rate rule,
      described above.

      With Chobble Tickets, the organiser holds the relationship. Attendee
      data is encrypted with the organiser's key, exports as CSV, and can
      be erased on request. Chobble does not run a marketplace, does not
      advertise to attendees, and its income comes from the annual
      hosting fee organisers pay.
