      # Chobble Tickets vs Weezevent

      Weezevent is a French event platform covering ticketing, access control, cashless payments and staff management. This page compares it with Chobble Tickets on pricing, features and data handling.

      ## Open source & cheaper Weezevent alternative

      Chobble Tickets is a white label ticketing platform made in the UK by a
      community interest company. Its source code is published under the
      AGPL-3.0-only licence, so anyone can read it, fork it, or run their own
      copy. Weezevent does not publish its source code.

      Weezevent charges 2.5% + £0.99 including tax on each paid ticket sold
      online, with banking fees included. Chobble Tickets charges £50/year or
      £5/month, and payment processing is separate.

      On a £15 ticket that is £1.37 to Weezevent. Taking Stripe's UK rate of
      1.5% + 20p as the processing cost, an organiser selling more than about
      55 tickets at £15 in a year pays less with Chobble Tickets. Square or
      SumUp rates give a different figure.

      ## What is Weezevent?

      [Weezevent](https://weezevent.com/en-gb/) started in France in 2008 and
      now has around 260 staff across offices in France, Spain, Canada,
      Switzerland, Belgium, the UK, the Netherlands and Germany. It says it
      has been used by 500,000 event organisers. It also owns the UK ticketing
      company Kaboodle.

      The product is broader than ticketing. WeezTicket sells tickets,
      WeezAccess handles door scanning, WeezPay runs cashless wristbands and
      cards, WeezCrew manages volunteers and accreditation, and Gigz covers
      email, SMS and advertising.

      Its main customers are festivals, venues and large outdoor events.
      Named clients include Tomorrowland, Live Nation and PSG.

      ## Pricing comparison

      Weezevent charges **2.5% + £0.99 including tax** on each paid ticket
      sold online. There is no setup fee and no subscription, and free events
      cost nothing. Banking fees are included in the ticket fee, so there is
      no separate card processing charge.

      On a £15 ticket that is **£1.37**. On a £30 ticket it is **£1.74**.
      Because most of the charge is fixed, the fee weighs more heavily on
      cheap tickets: on a £5 ticket it is £1.11, or 22% of the price.

      Chobble Tickets is a flat **£50/year or £5/month** (£25/year for
      charities and community groups on the annual plan) with
      [no per-ticket platform fees](/features/no-per-ticket-fees/). Stripe,
      Square or SumUp processing (1.5% + 20p) is paid on top. Selling more
      than about 55 tickets at £15 in a year costs less with Chobble Tickets.

      The other Weezevent products are priced separately. WeezAccess is free,
      WeezPay costs £1.20 per cashless transaction, and WeezCrew starts at
      £1,000.

      ## Payouts

      Weezevent pays ticket income out **every 15 days**. Money from a sale
      therefore sits with Weezevent until the next payout date, and the
      published pricing page does not describe an advance payout option.

      Chobble Tickets uses Stripe, Square or SumUp directly, so payments go
      straight to the organiser's own payment processor account. Payouts
      follow that processor's schedule, typically 2 to 3 business days from
      each sale, and the ticketing platform never holds the money.

      ## Data ownership and privacy

      Weezevent's [privacy policy](https://weezevent.com/en-gb/privacy-policy/)
      states that data is collected on behalf of the event organiser, who is
      the data owner, and that access is controlled through the organiser's
      back office. It says Weezevent may access the data when providing
      support. The policy does not set out whether Weezevent markets to
      attendees on its own behalf.

      The stated retention period for participant data is five years, or until
      a deletion request. Transaction records are kept for five years after
      the event as accounting documents.

      With Chobble Tickets, attendee names, email addresses, phone numbers,
      addresses and custom answers are encrypted before storage, and the
      organiser's key unlocks them. Chobble does not use attendee records for
      advertising or to market other events, as described on the
      [no attendee marketing](/features/no-spying/) page.

      ## Honest advertising

      Ticketing is a crowded market, and some of the promotion in it is not
      what it appears to be. Search results, review sites, "best of" lists and
      forum recommendations are all places where paid or undisclosed promotion
      turns up.

      Five Reddit accounts have posted about Weezevent across ticketing,
      event production, festival, conference, theatre, trade show, clubbing
      and small business subreddits during 2026. Weezevent is recommended in
      almost every post they have made.

      Some are long comparison articles ending on Weezevent, such as
      ["Eventbrite Alternative - 2026"](https://arctic-shift.photon-reddit.com/api/posts/ids?ids=1t5ci1u).
      Others are questions with Weezevent already named in them, and one
      account left
      [the same opening sentence on three different threads](https://arctic-shift.photon-reddit.com/api/comments/ids?ids=op3v9lb)
      on a single day. Several of the accounts describe their own use of
      Weezevent in ways that contradict their earlier posts.

      Reddit does not publish who is behind an account, so there is no way to
      establish from the outside who runs them or whether Weezevent knows
      about them. The
      [honest advertising](/features/honest-advertising/) page sets out the
      posts and the contradictions with links to each one.

      The wider pattern is easier to document. Ticketing companies buy search
      ads on competitors' names, pay for placement in "top 10 ticketing
      platform" articles, offer incentives for reviews, and publish comparison
      pages that quote their own fees favourably against out-of-date figures
      for everyone else.

      This page is a comparison page written by a competitor, so it is not a
