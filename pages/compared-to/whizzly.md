      # Chobble Tickets vs Whizzly

      Whizzly is a UK platform for service bookings, online shops and takeaway orders. This page compares its prices, features and data handling with Chobble Tickets.
  - type: markdown
    content: |
      ## Open source & flat fee Whizzly alternative

      Whizzly and Chobble Tickets earn their money the same way: a
      subscription paid by the business, with no commission on sales.
      Whizzly charges 0% platform commission on every plan. Chobble
      Tickets takes no per-ticket fee, and buyers pay the organiser's own
      Stripe, Square or SumUp account.

      The subscriptions differ in shape. Whizzly splits its tools across
      two plans and around thirty powerup add-ons costing £1.49 to £9.99
      a month, with metered AI tokens and WhatsApp message credits.
      Chobble Tickets includes every current feature in one prepaid
      price, with no add-on charges and no metered usage.

      [Chobble Tickets is open source](/features/open-source/): the
      complete product is published under the AGPL-3.0-only licence, and
      the same code can be run on the organiser's own infrastructure
      with no Chobble software fee. Whizzly is proprietary software, and
      its terms reserve all platform intellectual property for
      Whizzly Ltd.
  - type: markdown
    content: |
      ## A booking diary or a ticketed event

      Whizzly's booking tools are built around a salon-style diary. Each
      service has a duration, and Whizzly inserts cleanup buffers of 10
      to 30 minutes between appointments. The diary syncs two ways with
      Google, Apple and Outlook calendars, so personal blocks and lunch
      breaks close bookable slots. One client books one slot, pays a
      deposit, and receives reminders by WhatsApp and email.

      Chobble Tickets is built for listings with a date and a capacity,
      from workshops and gigs to classes and hire bookings. Attendees
      buy tickets, receive a QR code and are scanned in at the door, and
      a booking uses capacity only when its payment is confirmed.

      The platforms overlap for organisers selling places in sessions.
      A studio running group classes could sell them on either
      platform, and so could a tutor or a club. The diary tools suit
      one-to-one work, and the ticket tools suit anything with a door
      and a headcount.

      ## Payment processing

      Whizzly takes no commission and does not hold the money. Card
      payments run through Stripe Connect into the merchant's own
      Stripe account, paid out on Stripe's normal schedule. Stripe
      charges its processing fee directly, at 1.5% + 20p for standard
      UK cards according to Whizzly's pricing FAQ, with more for
      premium and international cards. Cash on collection and bank
      transfer are also supported.

      Refunds are the merchant's job. The terms make each merchant
      responsible for customer service, returns, refunds,
      cancellations and disputes, in line with the Consumer Rights
      Act 2015.

      On Chobble Tickets, buyers pay the organiser's own Stripe,
      Square or SumUp account, and that provider's normal rates apply
      on top of the hosting price. [Refunds](/features/refunds/) are
      issued from the admin panel against the original payment.

      ## Deposits and no-shows

      Whizzly's headline booking feature is the upfront deposit. A
      fixed amount or a percentage is set per service, and the client
      pays it through Apple Pay or Google Pay when booking. The deposit
      is deducted from the final bill, and the remaining balance is
      collected in the chair through a QR code payment. Whizzly's
      pricing page names the feature "Upfront Non-Refundable Deposit
      Protection".

      Chobble Tickets takes deposits in three forms: a percentage of
      the order, a flat amount per order, or an amount per item. A
      booking uses capacity only when the deposit payment is
      confirmed. The outstanding balance is tracked in a ledger, and
      the customer receives a secure link to pay it online.

      ## Data ownership

      Whizzly's privacy policy makes each merchant the data controller
      for their storefront customers and Whizzly a data processor
      acting on the merchant's instructions. It records customer names,
      email addresses, WhatsApp numbers, delivery addresses, allergy
      instructions, order and appointment details, loyalty balances and
      marketing opt-ins.

      The same policy lists the sub-processors involved: Google
      Firebase and Cloud Firestore, Stripe, PayPal, Meta's WhatsApp
      Cloud API, Google Cloud and its Gemini AI, Google Analytics,
      Meta and TikTok advertising pixels, and Resend for email. Several
      process data in the United States under Standard Contractual
      Clauses.

      Storefront customer data is retained for the lifetime of the
      merchant's active account unless anonymisation is requested.
      Merchant account data is kept for 30 days after cancellation,
      and financial records for seven years to meet HMRC requirements.
      Merchants can download a complete JSON archive of their account
      and store data from the dashboard, and customers can ask a store
      for a copy of their data or its deletion.

      With Chobble Tickets, attendee data is encrypted at rest. The
      organiser downloads a [complete database
      backup](/features/backups/), exports [attendee records as
      CSV](/features/csv-export/) and moves event setup between hosts
      as versioned JSON. Chobble's income comes from the hosting fee
      organisers pay, and attendees receive no marketing from the
      platform.

      ## Ending a paid term

      Whizzly subscriptions renew automatically each billing cycle
      until cancelled. Cancellation takes one click in the dashboard or
      the Stripe customer portal, and the storefront stays live until
      the end of the period already paid for. Subscription fees and
      one-off booster purchases are non-refundable.

      After cancellation, or when a trial ends without a paid plan, the
      storefront is deactivated. Catalogue and customer data is kept
      for 30 days to allow renewal or CSV export, after which Whizzly
      may delete it permanently.

      Chobble Tickets is [prepaid](/features/prepay-hosting/): a term
      is paid for in advance, no card is stored, and nothing renews on
      its own. When a term ends without renewal, the site switches to
      read-only mode for 24 months, during which every record and
      export stays available while new bookings stop. Paying again
      during that window restarts the site without charge for the
      read-only months, and after the 24 months the site and its
      database are deleted.

      ## How the software is built

      The marks on Whizzly's public pages point to software built quickly
      with AI assistance. Its blog posts carry bylines such as "Whizzly
      Product & Commerce Team", the terms had reached version 4.3 and the
      privacy policy version 3.3 by October 2026, and the accept-bookings
      page's live demo buttons link to `http://lashes-by-leona.localhost:3000`,
      a local development address left on the published page (checked 24
      October 2026). No status page, changelog or engineering documentation
      appears in the site's sitemap.

      Whizzly's pages ship Firebase code to the browser, which connects
      directly to the platform's Cloud Firestore database, and the privacy
      policy says tenant data is isolated by Firestore security rules keyed
      to each merchant's Firebase account.

      None of this shows a defect. It shows how the software was made and
      how new it is, which is worth weighing when a platform takes deposits
      and holds a booking calendar. The [guide to non-vibe-coded ticket
      sales
      systems](/guide/choosing-a-ticket-platform/non-vibe-coded-ticket-sales-systems/)
      sets out the risks unreviewed code brings to payment and availability
      logic, and how to check for them.
